01

The problem

Most amateur leagues are run by a volunteer — a commissioner, a bar owner, a club captain — on a spreadsheet, a group text, and whatever payment app people already have. The work is small and constant: move a game, fix a score, chase the team that hasn’t paid, email a division. Existing tools make them learn a product. With Beleeg they just say what they want, and lebrAIn does the rest.

02

The product

A React web app and an Expo mobile app over one shared TypeScript core — scoring, standings, the schedule generator, and the AI action registry are written once and used by both.

Run the season

A nine-step setup, schedule generation, lineups, live scoring, standings with configurable tiebreakers, playoffs, and tournament brackets.

Everyone gets their view

Home pages shaped for admins, captains, players, fans, media, and referees, plus public pages for every league, team, and organization.

Money moves correctly

Registrations, team dues, tournament entries, stores, and referee payouts through Stripe Connect, landing in the organizer’s own account.

Messages that arrive once

Email, SMS, and push through one queue with idempotency keys, a retry ladder, and a single policy for quiet hours and preferences.

Your own domain

Organizations can white-label Beleeg on their own domain; the app resolves which organization a request belongs to from the host it arrives on.

Moving a real league in

An idempotent import took a live league from its old Firestore app into Beleeg’s Postgres — deterministic IDs, so it can be re-run safely.

Hosting a tournament takes a name and a list of players: four formats, including a second-chance bracket and a three-match guarantee, with entry fees paid straight into the organizer’s Stripe.

03

lebrAIn, the league copilot

lebrAIn is docked on every signed-in page. Talk to it, type to it, or show it a photo: it already knows the league — teams, schedule, standings, who has paid — and it runs on Claude. Every request follows the same fast path from a sentence to done:

Ask Plain language

“It’s pouring — postpone tonight’s games.” The model can only choose from actions the registry exposes for this page and this person.

Check Server-side roles

Every action declares the roles allowed to take it, and the server filters on the caller’s real roles — never on what the client claims. A player can’t reschedule the league by asking nicely.

Prepare Nothing has changed yet

The action returns a plain summary, a preview of exactly what will change, and a ticket that expires in ten minutes. A stale approval is refused.

Confirm A person taps yes

Only the confirm step writes — and for actions that send email or open a Stripe checkout, it performs the delivery and reports what actually happened.

04

What you can ask it

Every one of these is a real action in lebrAIn’s registry — each a sentence away:

“It’s pouring — call off tonight”

Postpones every unplayed match on the day in one action, then pulls up the open dates for makeups.

“Who still hasn’t paid?”

Finds every unpaid member and sends one reminder to all of them, or records a payment made outside Beleeg.

“Settle the Hawks score”

Resolves a disputed result, records a forfeit, or nudges a captain who hasn’t confirmed.

“Build the schedule”

Generates a full round-robin season, blocks out holidays and closures, and reschedules any match on request.

“Put Sam on the Owls”

Moves players, assigns captains and referees, invites up to 200 people at once, and fills spots from the waitlist.

“Start next season”

Rolls the league into a new season, schedules an announcement, or adds a social event with RSVPs.

05

Run your league from Meta Muse, Claude, or Cursor

Beleeg ships an MCP server, so an organizer can run the league from whichever assistant they already use. When Meta opened Muse to third-party connectors on September 18, 2026, Beleeg was submitted to Muse’s directory the very next day — among the first apps to apply. Two days later a real Muse conversation created a league on Beleeg, start to finish.

Every action, in every assistant

The server is generated from the same registry as lebrAIn: one tool per action, 43 in all, plus the lookups that answer questions about schedules, standings, and who’s paid.

One tap still decides

Outside assistants use the same prepare-and-confirm path, so Muse shows the organizer exactly what will happen — and asks — before it starts a league or sends a message.

Connect in a minute

Hosted at beleeg.com/api/mcp over streamable HTTP, with scoped access tokens an organizer creates in settings and pastes into their assistant.

06

Multi-tenant by default

Organizations own leagues, leagues own seasons, and permissions inherit down that tree. The database is default-deny: application data is reached through security-definer functions that check the caller, and audit scripts read the live schema to prove every one of those functions authenticates and every write stays inside its tenant. The schema is 136 migrations deep; the backend is Supabase Postgres plus 47 Deno edge functions.
